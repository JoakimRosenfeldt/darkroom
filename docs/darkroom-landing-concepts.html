Version 0.1.0 / macOS, Windows, Linux

A photo library that does not move your photos.

Pick a folder. Darkroom indexes it, edits it, and exports from it. Your directory structure is the library.

Everything runs on the machine in front of you. No account, no sync, no upload, no subscription to keep your own catalog readable.

Download 0.1.0 Read the source GitHub
01

Scan

Choose a directory and Darkroom walks it recursively. Files are read on demand, thumbnails are cached by path and modification time, and the grid stays smooth because it only draws the rows you can see.

02

Sort

Flags, five stars, five color labels, keywords, albums and smart albums that follow a rule instead of a list. Compare two frames side by side and pick the sharper one.

03

Develop

Exposure, tone, curves, HSL, color grading, point color, detail, optics, crop and upright. Brush, linear and radial masks. Subject and sky selection from a model that runs offline once you have it.

04

Deliver

Copy settings from one frame and paste them onto a selection, or queue a batch. Batch jobs are written to disk as they run, so quitting halfway does not throw away the work.

Nothing you do here is destructive.

Adjustments live in a per folder JSON catalog and in XMP sidecars written beside each photo. The original file is opened for reading and never rewritten.

History is per photo and undoable. Versions are saved separately, so a second crop does not overwrite the first one.

What it opens, and what it refuses.

Formats it cannot decode are still recognized. The file appears with the reason it cannot be opened rather than disappearing from the grid.

Desktop editor / 0.1.0

Edit the picture. Leave the file alone.

Darkroom is a desktop editor for photographers who already have a folder structure they like and would rather not hand it to someone else's cloud.

Download for macOS Windows and Linux builds Read the source

One frame

The whole window gives way to the photograph.

Open a frame and the interface steps back. Filmstrip along the bottom, metadata on the side when you want it, zoom on click, and hold the backslash key to see the original underneath your edit.

Adjustments render through WebGL2 as you drag the slider. There is a CPU path for machines where that is not available.

Four hundred frames

Then it gets out of the way of the boring part.

Select a run from a shoot, apply a preset, and let the batch run. Progress is written to disk as it goes, so closing the laptop does not cost you the queue.

Copy settings from one photo and paste them onto a selection. Camera aware defaults mean a body you shoot often starts closer to where you want it.

Your edits are a text file next to your photo.

XMP sidecars and a local JSON catalog

Reads

NEF, JPEG, PNG and WebP. Anything else is named and explained rather than dropped.

Exports

JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF and TIFF, with resize and quality per job.

Masks

Brush, linear and radial, plus subject and sky from a model that runs on your machine.

Network

One optional download for the mask models. After that it works with the wifi off.

Version 0.1.0 for macOS, Windows and Linux

Still opens in ten years.

Darkroom keeps your library in the place you already keep it: folders, on your own disk. The catalog is JSON. The edits are XMP files sitting next to your photos. There is nothing here that stops working when a company changes its mind.

No account. No subscription. No upload.

Your folders, unchanged

Pick a directory and Darkroom indexes it recursively. Files are read on demand and never moved, renamed or copied into a hidden library.

A real develop module

Exposure and tone, curves, HSL, color grading, point color, detail, optics, crop and upright. Brush, linear and radial masks, with subject and sky selection that runs on your own machine.

Batches that survive a crash

Apply a preset to a whole shoot. The job is written to disk as it runs, so quitting the app in the middle costs you nothing but the current frame.

Look inside the catalog. It is a text file.

Darkroom stores one catalog.json per folder root and writes an .xmp sidecar beside each edited photo. Both are readable with any text editor.

If this project disappears tomorrow, your originals are untouched and your edits are still legible.

Reasonable questions

Does it upload my photos?

No. The only network request the app makes is an optional one time download of the subject and sky mask models. Skip it and Darkroom never touches the network.

What if I stop using it?

Your photos are where you left them, unmodified. The edits are XMP sidecars and a JSON catalog, both plain text. Delete them and you are back to the raw files.

Which cameras work?

Nikon NEF today, plus JPEG, PNG and WebP. Canon, Sony, Fujifilm and DNG files are recognized and listed with the reason they cannot be decoded yet, rather than being quietly hidden.

Is it finished?

No. It is version 0.1.0. Compare view, smart albums, keywords, presets, batch develop and export all work. Video does not exist and most raw formats are still missing.
